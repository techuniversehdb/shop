Buying Guide

Five simple rules: (1) Set a clear budget. (2) Decide what matters most — camera, battery or gaming. (3) Check how many years of software updates the phone gets. (4) Buy from a trusted shop with a GST bill. (5) Hold the phone before buying — feel the weight and check the screen in person.

Buy the latest only if you need the new features. Last year's model often gives almost the same experience at a noticeably lower price. New models cost more at launch — older models drop in price within months. Check how many update years are left before deciding.

At minimum: a screen guard and a back cover. Apply the screen guard at the time of purchase. Get a good quality charger and cable. Avoid very cheap chargers — they can damage the battery. A power bank is useful if you are a heavy user.

Check the IMEI, screen, touch, cameras, speakers, charging port, battery health, Wi-Fi and SIM slots. Test every button. Ask for the original bill and box. Make sure the phone is not locked to someone's Google or Apple account. Get a receipt with the seller's details.

The one that fits your budget, use case and nearby service centre. Samsung and Apple are known for long software support. Redmi and Realme offer the best value for tight budgets. Vivo and OPPO are popular for cameras and selfies. OnePlus and iQOO are preferred for speed and gaming. Visit Tech Universe to compare all brands in one place.
Phone Specs

4 GB — minimum for basic use (calls, WhatsApp, YouTube).
6–8 GB — suits most people including moderate gamers.
12 GB+ — for heavy gaming and heavy multitasking.

iPhones run well with less RAM because of software optimisation. The processor matters as much as RAM.

For all-round photography and video — iPhone and Samsung Galaxy. For portraits and selfies — Vivo and OPPO. For budget cameras — Redmi and Realme offer solid value. Visit Tech Universe to compare real photos taken on current models before you decide.

iQOO and OnePlus are well known for gaming performance. Look for Snapdragon 7 or 8 series (or Dimensity equivalent), 8 GB+ RAM, a 90–120 Hz screen and good thermal management. Fast charging also helps for long gaming sessions.

Capacity decides how long it lasts — fast charging decides how quickly you refill. Most people do best with 5000–6000 mAh and 33W or more fast charging. Modern phones manage fast charging well and it does not significantly harm battery health.
